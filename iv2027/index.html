Introduction

Foundation models, vision-language-action (VLA) models and world models are increasingly used in intelligent vehicles, and end-to-end driving is becoming a realistic alternative to modular pipelines. In real traffic, however, these vehicles face distribution shift, degraded GNSS and sensor data, limited corner-case data and uncertain behaviour of other road users, and a learned policy may produce wrong actions without any warning.

For safe operation, a vehicle needs to estimate the uncertainty of its outputs, monitor the integrity of its localization and perception, and switch to a safe fallback when its confidence is low. This workshop invites researchers working on foundation models and robot learning, together with researchers in localization, perception, 3D scene reconstruction, planning, human factors and safety, to discuss how learning-based intelligent vehicles can be made robust, transparent and safe in urban environments.

The half-day program includes invited talks, spotlight talks and a poster session for accepted papers, small-group discussions with the speakers during the coffee break, and a panel discussion.

Themes and Topics

1. Foundation models, world models and scene reconstruction End-to-end driving and VLA models, multimodal scene understanding, 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF for mapping and digital twins, and generative simulation of corner cases.
2. Trustworthy autonomy Uncertainty quantification, out-of-distribution detection, integrity monitoring of positioning and perception, runtime verification, safe fallback and certification of learned components.
3. Human-centred and connected autonomy Intent prediction of road users, shared control and takeover, explainability, V2X and cooperative perception.
4. Deployment and evaluation Closed-loop testing in reconstructed scenes, sim-to-real transfer, benchmarks and datasets, onboard computing limits, and low-cost autonomy for less-structured traffic.

Call for Papers

We invite papers on learning-based intelligent vehicles, including ground vehicles, UAVs and other autonomous platforms. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to, the following.

  • Foundation, VLA and world models for perception, prediction, planning and control
  • 3D scene reconstruction (3DGS, NeRF), digital twins and generative simulation for testing and data generation
  • Uncertainty quantification, out-of-distribution detection and failure prediction
  • Integrity monitoring of GNSS/multi-sensor localization and perception in degraded environments
  • Runtime verification, safe fallback and certification of learned components
  • Human intent modelling, shared control, takeover and explainability
  • V2X, cooperative perception and connected autonomy
  • Closed-loop benchmarks, datasets and sim-to-real evaluation
  • Affordable and robust autonomy for emerging economies and mixed traffic
